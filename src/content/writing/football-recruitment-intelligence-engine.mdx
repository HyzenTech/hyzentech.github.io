<Step id="population">

Before there is a shortlist, there is a season: **303 profiles for 295 players**. A player can have more than one team spell.

Position and exposure organize that search space. Only **139 profiles** pass baseline eligibility. The dim marks remain visible so the exclusions are part of the story.

<Technical title="Eligibility and coverage">
  Baseline eligibility requires 450 minutes, 60% dominant positional exposure,
  and ten complete peers for a comparison. Groups reflect the largest observed
  positional exposure, not learned tactical roles. AM/CM comparisons are
  suppressed where peer support is insufficient; goalkeeper coverage is
  distribution only.
</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="profiles">

The centre backs stay. The other groups recede.

Now their positions on the canvas mean something: **long passes and interceptions per 90 minutes**. Sharing a position does not mean sharing a profile. These two dimensions show differences in recorded activity; the similarity calculation will use twelve.

<Technical title="What this view can tell us">
  Coordinates use actual values from eligible centre-back profiles. Per-90 rates
  use validated minutes. This is a two-metric scatterplot, not a projection of
  the full similarity space. Event activity alone does not establish tactical
  role, defensive quality, or team-context-adjusted ability.
</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="pipeline">

Follow **Katrine Veje’s Everton profile** back to its evidence.

The pipeline validates and aggregates match events, then uses playing time to calculate comparable rates. Veje’s profile records **1.11 interceptions per 90**. It emerges from the data rather than a manually assigned player type.

<Technical title="Event flow and normalization">
  The fixed WSL 2023/24 source contains 132 matches and 495,189 events. Event
  marks here are schematic; they do not represent an event sample or event
  frequencies. The released pipeline derives 41 metrics. Veje’s twelve active
  similarity features come from that larger profile. Per-90 normalization and
  later candidate-population standardization are separate operations.
</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="similarity">

Veje becomes the reference point. **31 complete peers** spread out by their distance from her profile.

Josie Green is the closest, at **2.44**. That answers “who resembles this profile?” It says nothing yet about what a club needs next.

<Technical title="How distance is calculated">
  Candidate-only standardized Euclidean distance uses twelve active features,
  omitting zero-variance dimensions. The query does not enter the scaler.
  Same-player team spells are excluded. The engine’s display index is 100 / (1 +
  distance), not a probability. Horizontal position shows the exact distance;
  vertical packing separates overlapping marks and carries no analytical
  meaning.
</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="brief">

**The closest profile is not automatically the best fit for a brief.**

Keep five real candidates in similarity order. Now introduce the stored centre-back brief: interceptions, tackles, and long passes, weighted **3 : 2 : 1**.

</Step>

<Step id="ranking">

The same candidates change places.

Green goes from **closest profile to brief rank 10**. Buchanan goes from **similarity rank 14 to brief rank 1**. No player changed; the question did.

<Technical title="Ranking method and the five examples">
  Direction-aware midrank percentiles use a common complete reference population
  of 32 centre-back candidates. Normalize the weights, then sum the percentile
  contributions. Candidate filters apply after the reference is established. The
  five displayed players are a selected subset: rank gaps are real. The stored
  brief expresses project conventions, not validated scouting preferences.

</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="explanation">

Buchanan’s **91.67** is a sum, not a verdict.

Interceptions contribute **49.22** points; tackles, **32.81**; long passes, **9.64**. The smaller final bar reflects both a lower percentile and a lighter weight. The score stays inspectable.

<Technical title="Read the contribution, not an ability rating">
  Buchanan’s percentiles are 98.4375 for interceptions, 98.4375 for tackles, and
  57.8125 for long passes. Their normalized weights are 1/2, 1/3, and 1/6. The
  unrounded contributions sum to 91.6666667. Displayed values are rounded; this
  score applies to this reference population and brief only.
</Technical>

</Step>

<Step id="system">

**Make the evidence travel with the result.**

Validated data becomes profiles, comparisons, a local API, and reproducible artifacts. This is decision support. Independent scouting and predictive validation remain unperformed.

</Step>
