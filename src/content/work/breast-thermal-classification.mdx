## What is this?

This research examines whether thermal-image patterns can help distinguish Healthy, Benign, and Malignant classes in a curated DMR dataset. CNNs extract representations from the images, and XGBoost classifies those representations.

It is an experimental classification study, not a clinically validated diagnostic product.

## The problem and why it matters

Images contain many more values than a researcher can assess individually. The central idea is to learn useful visual features, then evaluate whether those features separate the three classes. Thermal imaging records temperature patterns; the research investigates those patterns rather than treating them as a diagnosis by themselves.

## Dataset

The existing research write-up reports a DMR source of 5,483 images from 425 patients. The curated experiment used 1,842 images: 614 per class.

| Split      | Per class | Total |
| ---------- | --------- | ----- |
| Training   | 430       | 1,290 |
| Validation | 123       | 369   |
| Test       | 61        | 183   |

These are the numbers recorded in the project write-up. Patient-level separation and broader clinical generalization must be established from the study protocol before making stronger claims.

## Approach and architecture

![CNN feature extraction followed by XGBoost classification.](/images/blog-cnn-xgboost.webp)

VGG16 and ResNet50 provide two feature-extraction approaches. XGBoost then operates on those features. Comparing the pipelines helps separate the choice of representation from the classifier.

## Experiments and results

| Pipeline           | Training accuracy reported in article | Validation accuracy                                      |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| VGG16 + XGBoost    | 87.44%                                | 72.89% in both existing pages                            |
| ResNet50 + XGBoost | 99.45%                                | 85.63% in the article; 73.17% on the original About page |

> The existing sources disagree about ResNet50 validation accuracy. Both values are preserved with their source labels. Neither is presented here as a reconciled, independently verified headline result.

The difference between training and validation is important: a strong fit to training images does not, by itself, show generalization. No test-set accuracy is invented here.

## Limitations

The curated dataset and reported split constrain the interpretation. Balanced image counts do not establish patient independence, clinical sensitivity, external validity, or a deployment-ready screening tool. The conflicting validation metric must be checked against the final paper and experiment records.

## What I learned

Reporting the method and uncertainty together is part of the result. The useful research story is not just which architecture produces a larger number, but which evidence supports that comparison.
