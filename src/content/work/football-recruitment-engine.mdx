## What is this?

A recruitment tool should help someone ask a better question about a player. This system turns a pinned season of event data into player profiles, comparable peers, and explainable rankings. It keeps the source, assumptions, and limitations next to the result.

The finished V1 is a local analytical product with a FastAPI interface. The screenshot shows the actual interface before selecting a query. The interactive example on this portfolio teaches the scoring arithmetic; it is not connected to the player database.

## The problem

A player can resemble someone you already know and still be a poor match for your recruitment brief. Similarity asks, “Who has a comparable statistical profile?” Ranking asks, “Who meets these priorities?” Combining those questions without explaining the difference can make an attractive shortlist misleading.

## My role

I developed the analytical pipeline, API, interface, validation, and reproducible release. The goal was an inspectable baseline that could be reviewed before adding predictive features.

## Approach

Start with events, build player–team–season profiles, establish eligible same-position peers, and then separate similarity from recruitment ranking. A result includes its evidence and contribution breakdown rather than an unexplained overall score.

## Architecture

![Source data passes through validation, profiles and query services to the local interface.](/images/recruitment-architecture.svg)

The Python pipeline creates a reproducible analytical snapshot. FastAPI and Pydantic expose validated queries to the interface. Source versions and artifact hashes support provenance.

<Technical title="Technical decisions: peers, similarity, and ranking">
Similarity uses candidate-only standardized Euclidean distance. Zero-variance dimensions are omitted. The displayed index is 100 / (1 + distance); it is not a probability. A query excludes the same player's other team spells.

Ranking uses weighted percentiles from a common, complete reference population. Candidate filters apply after establishing that reference. This distinction prevents a UI filter from silently changing the meaning of a percentile.

</Technical>

## Data

The pinned StatsBomb WSL 2023/24 snapshot contains 132 matches and 495,189 events. Processing yields 303 player–team–season profiles, representing 295 players. Of those profiles, 139 meet basic eligibility.

These are counts from the released pipeline, not population-wide claims. The project implements 41 metrics and eight presets.

<Technical title="Eligibility and missing evidence">
  The baseline requires 450 minutes, 60% dominant positional exposure, and at
  least ten complete peers for the applicable comparison. Some AM and CM groups
  have too few peers, so the system suppresses unsupported comparisons.
  Goalkeeper coverage is distribution only.
</Technical>

## Implementation and results

V1 was frozen with 220 tests; six later export checks bring the source validation total to 226. Two clean builds checked reproducibility. Twenty perturbations examined stability: striker similarity was available in 202 of 280 checks, and striker ranking in 18 of 20.

These checks establish software behavior and evidence boundaries. They do not establish scouting accuracy or predictive performance.

## Limitations

One centre-back qualitative review, CASE-03, remains limited. Its sample contains 30 observations, with unknown continuous viewing duration. Expert/scout validation and predictive validation have not been performed.

Age, price, contract, injury, availability, team context, and league adjustments are absent. A shortlist still needs football judgment and additional evidence. The code's MIT license does not replace provider data rights.

## What I learned

A complete pipeline is only useful if the meaning of its outputs survives filtering, missing peers, and reproduction. Clear suppression is a better product behavior than returning a confident-looking number without enough evidence.

## What's next
